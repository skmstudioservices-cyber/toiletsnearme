Toilets at petrol pumps — the highway rest stop explained

Page last updated 29 September 2026

On Indian highways, the fuel station is the default rest stop — and most of the bigger ones do have a restroom.

Quick answerMost larger Indian petrol pumps have toilets — especially highway plazas and company outlets (IndianOil, BP, HP) with food courts. Condition varies widely: dedicated highway plazas are usually clean, small city pumps can be basic. You don't need to buy fuel to ask for the restroom. For a cleaner, guaranteed option, nearby pay-and-use complexes and the next town's mall work better.

Which pumps have restrooms

  • Highway plazas with food courts: the best bet — built for stopovers.
  • Company-owned outlets: most keep a staff or customer restroom.
  • Small dealer pumps: hit-or-miss; ask the attendant.

What to expect

Highway plazas maintain restrooms as part of their food business; standalone pumps range from functional to squat-only. Carry tissues and sanitiser on long drives — supplies are not guaranteed.

Alternatives on the highway

Sulabh pay-and-use complexes at town entries, mall restrooms in bigger towns, and toll-plaza facilities on expressways. Our highway etiquette: plan stops every 100–150 km rather than hoping the next pump works out.

❓ Toilets at petrol pumps — FAQ

Do petrol pumps in India have toilets?

Most larger outlets — especially highway plazas and company-run pumps — do; small dealer pumps vary, so ask the attendant.

Are petrol pump toilets free?

Usually free, sometimes ₹5–10 at highway plazas that fund an attendant.

Can I use a petrol pump toilet without buying fuel?

Yes — asking for the restroom is routine and accepted at Indian fuel outlets.

Are highway petrol pump toilets clean?

Plazas with food courts are usually well kept; standalone pumps can be basic. Carry tissues and sanitiser.

What are better highway alternatives?

Toll-plaza facilities, Sulabh complexes at town entries, and mall restrooms in larger towns along the route.
