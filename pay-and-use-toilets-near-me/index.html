What pay-and-use means

A pay-and-use toilet is a public convenience charging a per-use fee that funds its attendant, water supply and cleaning. Sulabh complexes and municipal community toilet complexes are the most common operators.

What you get for the fee

  • An attendant and scheduled cleaning — usually the cleanest public option
  • Running water and sometimes bathing facilities
  • Separate sections for men and women
  • Fixed, posted hours — some stay open 24 hours

❓ Pay and use toilets near me — FAQ

What is a pay and use toilet?

A public toilet charging a small per-use fee — usually ₹5–10 — that pays for its attendant, water and cleaning. Sulabh complexes and municipal bodies run most of them.

How much does a pay and use toilet cost in India?

Typically ₹5–10 per use; many complexes also sell inexpensive monthly passes for regular users.

Are pay and use toilets cleaner than free ones?

Generally yes — the fee funds an attendant and regular cleaning, which is why they are the most dependable public option.

Do pay and use toilets stay open at night?

Some do — especially at railway stations and major junctions. Check the posted hours; our open-now guide lists reliable late-night options.

What if I don't have change?

Many complexes accept UPI now; attendants can often make change during the day. Railway-station facilities are often the most flexible.
