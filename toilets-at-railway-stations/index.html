Toilets at railway stations — what to expect and where

Page last updated 29 September 2026

Every railway station in India has toilets, and they stay open with the station — 24 hours. The catch is knowing which entrance and what condition.

Quick answerIndian railway stations have toilets on or near every platform, open around the clock with the station itself. They are mostly free at stations (premium waiting rooms and lounges charge). For long journeys, trains carry onboard bio-toilets. Find station facilities via the station's directory boards, or use our live map for nearby public toilets around the station area.

Where station toilets are

Toilets sit at platform level — usually near the overhead bridge, waiting hall or one end of the platform. Directory boards on concourses mark them, and railway staff will point you to the nearest set.

Cleanliness — the honest picture

Indian Railways has been retrofitting and outsourcing station cleanliness for years; major stations are now serviceable, smaller ones vary. Onboard, trains use bio-toilets, and premium trains add onboard housekeeping.

Free vs paid at stations

Platform toilets are generally free. Retiring rooms, AC waiting halls and executive lounges cost money but are cleaner. Non-passengers can usually use platform facilities — at bigger stations you may need a platform ticket to enter.

❓ Toilets at railway stations — FAQ

Do railway stations have toilets?

Yes — every station has toilets at or near platform level, and they are open as long as the station is (24 hours).

Are railway station toilets free?

Platform toilets are generally free; premium waiting rooms, retiring rooms and executive lounges charge.

Can non-passengers use station toilets?

Usually yes, though at major stations you may need a platform ticket to enter the station area.

Are Indian railway station toilets clean?

Major stations have improved with outsourced cleaning; smaller stations vary. Premium waiting areas are the cleanest option at a station.

Do trains have toilets on board?

Yes — Indian trains carry onboard toilets, now mostly bio-toilets, with housekeeping on premium trains.
